# R-SURFACE1 G3 — windscreen wipers and wet sheen

Status: **G3.3 BUILT (DLL d9774313), awaiting the owner's race review** (2026-11-13). G2.2 is accepted, and the PS2 reflection and fleet damage fixes (`docs/reflect-damage1`) are
confirmed in game. The wheel grime from the original G3 plan was already done in G2.1 (even tyre layer, rim patches).

## Windscreen wiper arcs

* **Detection** (`grimeshell::glass_frame`, at the first sight of a glass draw in a race):
  * the area-weighted outward normal of the draw's car-local triangles faces the front (+z > .35);
  * it does not face down (y > −.1) and is not a side window (|x| < .5);
  * it is wider than 30 cm.

  Side and rear windows keep the normal glass grime. It works for windows drawn by owned draws and for those under `$switch(screen*)` nodes, which
  `grime_switch_part` matches by WORLD.
* **Mask**: windscreen glass draws use a per-car 64×64 wiper texture on stage 1 instead of the body zone texture.
  * The mapping is the windscreen plane: u along car-local x, v from the top of the glass down (`wiper_matrix`).
  * Alpha is the windscreen level outside the arcs and 0 inside them. The level is `windscreen_level`: the zone level at the upper front of the body.
  * Two wipers pivot just below the bottom edge at u = .28 and .72 and sweep half-discs of radius .58 (`wiper_clean`, soft edge).
  * Outside the arcs the dust gathers at the edges and corners.
* `[Look] Wipers=1` (0 = windscreens like other glass).

## Wet sheen

* After the grime draw of a Body kind, when the car is wet (zone mean > .02), the pass draws the same geometry again, additive (ONE/ONE).
* It uses the body draw's own env stage: stage-1 texture, `TCI_CAMERASPACENORMAL` / `REFLECTIONVECTOR`, transform flags and the `TEXTURE1` matrix, read before the pass states are applied.
* The pass colour is TFACTOR grey = `Sheen` × wetness, so a car out of a water crossing shines and the shine fades as it dries (`DryTime`).
* Draws without an env stage get no sheen.
* Every state the sheen pass changes is in the recorded block, so the same save block restores it.
* `[Look] Sheen=0.35` (0 = off).
* The PS2 fleet bodies get sheen through the same pass when their mesh has an env stage (mode 3).

## G3.1 — wet sheen rework (owner review, 2026-11-13)

Owner: with the PC reflections (PS2 reflections switched off), a wet car only got brighter. Its reflection disappeared and it looked matte.
Cause: the G3 sheen ran in the post-draw grime pass, after the reflection scopes had ended. It added the static env texture at full strength on
top of the real reflection, so it washed the reflection out.

* The sheen moved to `grime_sheen()`, called through `grimeintegration::wet_sheen_hook` from `ps2integration::Scope::draw_result` (`reflection_native_integration.hpp`).
  That runs right after the native draw, while the draw's PC live reflection or PS2 reflection (texture, env coordinates, transform) is still applied.
  It runs for every draw, whether or not the PS2 reflection was applied.
* Pass: own state-block pair (`sheen_apply` / `sheen_save`, deleted before Reset).
  * stage 0 = TFACTOR × vertex alpha, the native env weight; TFACTOR = `Sheen` × wetness, × the Atmosphere night environment factor at night;
  * stage 1 = reflection × current;
  * ONE/ONE, z-write off, colour writes only.
* The grime pass no longer draws a sheen. The grime itself is drawn after the sheen, so dust still covers it.

## G3.2 — glass and full-car coverage (owner review, 2026-11-13)

Owner: the wet sheen looks right. No wiper trace was visible and it was unclear whether glass gets dirty at all; grime was only seen on the lower body.

* **Glass level**: `windscreen_level` is now half the car's mean zone amount plus half its dirtiest front zone (front low, front arch, bonnet).
  The G3 level, the zone level at the upper front of the body, stayed near 5 %, so glass looked clean and the arcs were invisible.
* **Side and rear glass**: every glass draw now uses the wiper texture. Windscreens use it in their glass plane (clean arcs). Other glass samples one point
  outside the arcs (`kGlassFilmU/V`), so it gets one even film at the glass level, patterned by the atlas noise.
* **Whole car**: the zone texel level is `amount × (shape + (1 − shape) × amount²)`.
  * Lightly dirty, the body shape still caps the roof and the upper body (the G1 look).
  * A zone near full dirt loses the cap, so a long dusty stage can cover the whole car.
  * Accumulation weights: bonnet .25 → .4, roof .1 → .2 (also in `start_state`).
* Tests: light dirt keeps the roof clean, full dirt covers it, glass levels, and the film point lies outside the arcs.

## Note: PS2 reflections on add-on cars (investigation)

* The PS2 reflection admits a draw only through `kReflectionMaterials`, a fixed table of exact geometry hashes of the retail cars' PS2 "carshiny"
  groups (`reflection_identity_for_hash`, `reflection_pilot.cpp`). Add-on cars never match, so they keep the PC reflection.
* The reflection itself is generic: the PS2 static term plus the framebuffer approximation on stage 1. It does not depend on the car.
* A second admission authority would extend it to any car: a fleet-owned draw of `vehicles/<family>/car` whose BodyMaterials role is paint
  (registry or naming contract) and which has the opaque env stage. All other admission rules (live race, native states, textures) stay as they are.
* Not implemented yet.

## G3.3 — smoother dry-to-wet transition (owner review, 2026-11-13)

Owner: the change from a dry to a wet car was too abrupt. The wet state changes in 0.5 s steps, and a water crossing wets a car in about a second.

* The displayed wetness of each car eases toward the simulated one (`ease_wet`: rise τ 2.5 s, dry τ 4 s, updated on every draw of the car). It drives
  both the tint darkening and the sheen.
* The sheen strength follows `sheen_curve` (smoothstep), so a damp car barely shines and a soaked car shines fully.
* Tests: the easing rates in both directions and the curve end points.
