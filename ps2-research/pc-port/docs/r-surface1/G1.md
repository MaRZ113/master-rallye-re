# R-SURFACE1 G1 — static grime look

Status: **G1.5 BUILT (DLL 4400ed96), awaiting the owner's race review** (2026-11-12). Every car gets the same fixed grime level
(`StartLevel`); dynamics come in G2.

## What it draws

One extra pass over each accepted car body draw, issued right after the draw and before the Atmosphere headlight pass, so
headlights also light the grime. Code:

| Part | File |
|---|---|
| Portable look (procedural noise atlas, zone ramp, matrices, TFACTOR) | `renderer/vehicle_grime_overlay.hpp` |
| Config, body candidates, managed textures (mip chain, created once) | `renderer/vehicle_grime_native.hpp` (`look()`, `note_body()`, `overlay_textures()`) |
| Candidate from the Atmosphere body route (paint, panels, trim, flat underbody, wheel covers of `vehicles/<f>/car`) | `renderer/atmosphere_body_general.hpp` |
| D3D pass (post-draw, `HeadlightPass` state transaction) | `renderer/atmosphere_native.hpp` `grime_overlay()`, called first in `sun_world` |
| Tests | `tests/ps2_diag_tests.cpp` `grime_overlay_tests()` |

Pass states, reusing the body draw's VB/IB/range/WORLD/fog:

* stage 0: colour = DIFFUSE (the lit vertex colour) × TFACTOR.rgb (tint); alpha = TFACTOR.a = level L. Stage 0 keeps its
  texture bound but does not read it.
* stage 1: 256² noise atlas through `TCI_CAMERASPACEPOSITION` × inverse(VIEW) × inverse(WORLD) × side projection.
  * Projection: u = L + 0.7 W, v = −Y + 0.4 W (length, width, height in car-local space; 3 tiles per car length). Front, rear and
    roof faces still get a 2D projection.
  * Colour: MODULATE2X detail. Alpha: ADDSIGNED2X(noise ∈ [0, .5], L) = 2(n + L − .5). At L = 0 nothing is drawn, at .5 only the noise
    peaks, and at 1 coverage is full. The grime grows from patches; it does not fade in.
* stage 2: 64×32 zone ramp normalised to the car bounds. The bounds are the union of the car's body draws. The ramp alpha multiplies the
  result: roof .12 → upper .35 → lower .85, with +.22 on the front and rear plates and the wheel arches, capped at .9.
* SRCALPHA/INVSRCALPHA, z-write off, LESSEQUAL, alpha test off.

Never covered: glass, lamps, wheels, interior, crew. These roles are never noted. `Enabled=0` or `StartLevel=0` means no pass at
all, so the frame is identical to the game without grime. Bodies farther than `MaxDistance` (80) are skipped. Caps check: the pass needs 3 blend stages, MODULATE2X and
ADDSIGNED2X. Without them it is off and this is logged.

## Config (`VehicleGrime.ini`)

```ini
[VehicleGrime]
Enabled=1
StartLevel=0.7     ; 0 clean .. 1 full lower body
StartMud=0.3       ; dust -> mud tint mix
MaxDistance=80
[Look]
DustRGB=0.68,0.57,0.43
MudRGB=0.30,0.24,0.17
```

Log: `grime_look`, `grime_overlay_caps`, `grime_overlay_textures`, and `grime_overlay` every 600 frames (draws, skipped_far, cars).
PerfTiming category `grime`.

## Build-session smoke (attract, host-grime-g0-01)

* About 40 overlay draws per frame for 4 cars; caps and textures OK; `hresult` 0.
* Screenshots at L = 1 / mud 1 and at L = .7 / mud .3 show a dark lower body, patchy bumper and rear panel, and clean glass and lamps.
  Two tuning steps followed: the first projection streaked on the rear face, which was fixed by mixing W into u/v; dust was too dark (multiplied by
  the vertex light), so it is now lighter, and the roof weight was lowered to .12.

## Limits (next steps)

* Only the native body route (PC, SDK and add-on cars accepted by the Atmosphere body materials) is covered. PS2 fleet bodies
  (`fleet_vertices`) do not get the pass yet. Without Atmosphere `BodyPrototype=Materials` there is no grime.
* There are no wheels, no glass with a wiper arc and no wet sheen (G3). There is no per-car state yet; dynamics and persistence come in G2.

## G1.1 — owner review fixes (2026-11-12, DLL 6c4261fc, registry ba468c18)

Owner run: the look is good, especially on brown cars, and performance is acceptable. Problems: the crew was dirty; grime was missing on wheels, tyres, grille, lamps and glass;
the Navara logo stickers did not match the body; the Evo IV was neither lit by Atmosphere nor dirty.

* **Crew dirty**: the registry gives the crew suits, heads and add-on crew (`whitesuit`, `head`, `mr-crew-*`) the "flat" role, and seats the
  "paint" role. Fix: `crew_or_interior()` name exclusion for every grime kind.
* **Kinds** (`grimeoverlay::Kind`): Body; Wheel (rubber and rim in wheel-local space, uniform lower-body weight, spins with the wheel;
  tyres inside the car resource of imported cars use the body route); Glass ×0.6; Lamp ×0.55; Grille ×0.55 masked by the texture alpha (holes stay
  holes); Decal masked by the texture alpha (a sticker gets exactly the body's grime, which fixes the Navara logos). The blended and alpha-tested car draws
  come from `grime_blended()` in `atmosphere_body_general.hpp`. It only records a candidate, needs the car's body bounds, and skips additive glow.
* **Distance**: wheels are drawn within 60 % of `MaxDistance`; glass, lamps, grille and decals within 40 %.
* **Evo IV (GT4 import)**: its env textures are `gt4-env-<hash>` (paint) and `classic-rubber-env-<hash>` (tyres), which are not retail
  contract names. The registry builder left the paint unclassified, so Atmosphere and grime skipped the car. Fix: `env_alias()` in
  `BodyMaterials` (C++) and in `tools/atmosphere_vehicle_lighting.py`, applied before every role and safety check: rubber/tyre → rubber, glass → glass,
  chrome/metal → chrome, perspex/plastic → perspex, `-env-` → whitepaint. The runtime also derives roles for importer env names
  of registered families, so new imports need no registry rebuild. The rebuilt registry has 1366 records (Evo IV: 21 paint and 3 rubber rows);
  C++ and Python parity is 498/498.
* Attract smoke: body 52 k, wheels 48 k, glass 2.4 k and lamp 0.9 k overlay draws per 600-frame log; screenshots show dusty tyres and spare
  wheel, a light film on the rear glass and almost clean lamps.
* Owner perf (e452421c race): mostly divisor 3 (60 fps); grime about 1.0 ms per frame on average, 2.3 ms peak. The state transaction per pass is the main cost
  (G2 optimisation).

## G1.2 — owner review 2 (2026-11-12, DLL 1660b8b7)

Owner findings: driver faces were dirty; wheels were too bright and the tyre and rim merged into one fine, noisy blob; grime was missing on some grilles, on all
rear lamps, on some front lamps (Patrol) and on the Navara Nissan logo; the Lancer body had none, and its tyres looked gone.
An offline audit of every material in the registry report (`blm-addons-4/report.json`) against the grime rules found:

* **Faces** use texture names after the drivers (`dcambell`, `drichard`, `dbruno`, `dnissan*`) with the "flat" role. They are now excluded.
* **Lamps, the Nissan logo and some stickers** are OPAQUE draws with the registry role `native`: the lighting tool deliberately leaves lamps native.
  Only blended draws reached the fallback, so these were skipped. Now every car draw that the body route rejects goes through `grime_blended()`.
  The kind comes from the registry role (tyre → Wheel; rim, or any wheel-resource draw → Rim; paint-like roles → Body) or from the texture names. Lamp names are checked
  before glass, because lamp lenses often use the glass env.
* **Lancer**: its rims (`gt4-1805…`) use a paint env in the wheel resource and were native, so they are now the Rim kind. Its body has registry paint rows but
  never reaches the body route. The fallback now covers it: for a car that has no body bounds yet, the bounds are read once from the buffer shadow. The reason the
  Atmosphere lighting route rejects it is still unknown. `VehicleGrime.ini Census=1` logs `grime_census` once per material (state, role,
  kind, skip) to find it in a race with the Lancer.
* **Wheels**: separate kinds, tyre ×0.7 and rim ×0.4 (the rim stays readable), tone ×0.62 (darker), 0.8 atlas tiles per wheel (large blobs);
  grilles ×0.8 and lamps ×0.7 (they were too faint at ×0.55).
* **Performance**: the fallback first ran the owner stack walk for every course draw (grime 2.6–7 ms, 35–47 fps in attract). A per-race memo of
  draws proven not to be a car fixes this. The pass now uses two D3D8 state blocks (apply plus capture/restore) instead of a Get/Set/notify per state. Attract
  cost fell from 1.5–1.8 ms to 0.25–0.35 ms per frame (about 6 µs per pass; 80 fps windows again). The state blocks are deleted in `fleet_before_reset()`
  before a device Reset.

## G1.3 — owner review 3 (2026-11-12)

Owner findings: the small brake section of the rear lamps had no grime (seen on the Patrol); the Lancer Evo IV still had no grime, no Atmosphere
light, and its tyres were not drawn; there was some dust inside the Kia cabin and on the dark side of the drivers' helmets.
The owner's race log (`grime_census`, `atmosphere_body_material`, `atmosphere_instanced_material_unknown`) and offline model checks gave these causes:

* **Lancer Evo IV body.** The model has 95 root nodes. The fleet owner walk (`fleet_owner_uncached`, `race_context_uncached`) refused more than 64, so
  no body draw had an owner: no Atmosphere body light, no grime, nothing in the census. The limit is now `kOwnerRootLimit = 256`. The duplicate scan
  (quadratic) is gone, because a walk that must end on a null link within the limit already refuses a cyclic list. `FrontendOwner::meshes` still keeps the first 64.
* **Lancer Evo IV tyres.** The imported tread uses the same texture as a course tyre stack, and the car has no vehicle signature. The course resolver
  (`universal_draw`) relit the tread as a course prop, then the body route found that change pending and returned `D3DERR_DEVICELOST`, so the wrapper
  skipped the draw. This started in G1.1, when `env_alias` gave the tyres a rubber role. Two fixes:
  * `atmosphereintegration::vehicle_draw_probe` runs the fleet owner walk, with the answer cached per race and draw. Placed draws that belong to a car never reach the resolver
    (`resolver_fleet_owned_vehicle_draw_excluded`).
  * `general()` no longer fails a draw that another route already changed (`body_material_draw_owned_by_other_route`).
* **Headlamps.** The crew word `head` also matched `headlamp-tga`, so the Impreza and Navara front lamps were skipped. `head` now counts only when it is not followed by `l`.
* **Brake lens and windows under `$switch` nodes.** The blight-off brake lens (where the PS2 glow is built) and the windows sit under `$switch` nodes that the owner walk does not reach.
  * `grime_switch_part()` matches such a no-owner draw against the WORLD of the cars drawn earlier in the frame.
    * Opaque draws become Lamp, alpha-tested ones Decal, and SRCALPHA glass with the env stage becomes Glass.
    * Plain blended cards (glow layers) are skipped.
  * A draw that matches no car for 30 frames joins the not-car memo.
* **Cabin and helmets (shell).** Texture names cannot separate an inner door panel, a roll cage or the black helmet shell from outer black trim or paint. A flood fill
  of the cabin volume also fails: rally cabins are open, and in the prototype even three dilation passes left Kia air connected to the outside. The new test is sky visibility
  (`renderer/vehicle_grime_shell.hpp`):
  * The car-resource triangles of a model go into a 64-cell occupancy grid.
  * A triangle is exterior when at least 15 % of 32 rays in its outward hemisphere leave the grid. The outward normal is cross(c − a, b − a), which matches the authored normals.
  * Glow, flare and shadow cards are never occluders.

  Offline (Python prototype on the shipped models):
  * Crew, helmets, seats, the dash and the reflectors inside lamp housings score about 0.
  * Outer panels, stickers, the roof and the Navara pickup bed score 0.9–1.0.
  * The Kia roll cage (rubber role) is partly culled: its tubes are visible through the open windows.

  Runtime (`grime_shell` log):
  * The grid is built two frames after a model's first draw.
  * Masks are computed with a budget of 1 ms per frame, then kept for the process.
  * A draw gets no grime until its mask is ready, about the first second of a new model.
  * A draw with interior triangles is drawn from a 16-bit managed index arena (256 K indices) that holds only its exterior triangles, with the same VB, base vertex and range.
  * The `grime_overlay` log counts `subset` and `switch` draws; `grime_census` has a `shell` field.
* Tests: the headlamp words; a closed box with an inner block (all outer faces exterior, the inner block interior); degenerate triangles; no grid.

## G1.4 — owner review 4 (2026-11-12)

The owner confirmed that the Evo IV tyres are back and the Kia cabin is clean. New findings: grime ended abruptly around spare wheels (NewRav, Patrol, Tata),
many clean triangles on the Evo IV front, and dust too light.

* **Spare wheels.** The visibility was sampled at the centroid on the outward side only. A large rear-panel triangle whose centroid sits behind the spare wheel lost
  its grime entirely. Visibility is now the best of the centroid and three points 60 % of the way to the corners, each on both sides. The search stops at the first
  sample above the threshold, so exterior triangles cost one sample.
* **Evo IV front.** Imported faces with reversed winding looked inward, saw no sky, and were culled. They pass with the two-sided test.
* Offline (prototype `vis4.py`): crew, helmets and seats stay at about 0. False culls dropped:
  * NewRav rear panel 6 → 0, sides 25 → 8;
  * Patrol sides 20 → 9;
  * Lancer front panels by 2–5×.
* **Dust tint** default `DustRGB` .78,.66,.50 → .68,.57,.43 (template and host).
* Tests: a reversed outer face stays exterior; a reversed inner face stays interior.

## G1.5 — owner review 5 (2026-11-12)

* **Rear panels behind spare wheels** (Patrol and others) still lost grime: a tailgate spare wheel blocks the sky for the panel behind it. The shell
  now culls only inside the cabin box (`grimeshell::Cabin`): the car-local bounds of the crew and seat draws (`grimeoverlay::cabin_marker`, never
  underdash/interior), extended ±0.9 m along the length, from 0.5 m below them upwards, over the full width. The rest of the car always keeps its grime;
  a model without crew draws is never culled. `grime_shell` logs `cabin`.
* **Evo IV dust too light**: 9 of its paint materials are alpha-tested (GT4 cut-outs), and the body route took only draws without alpha test, so they stayed
  native-bright under Atmosphere. `general()` now relights alpha-tested opaque draws of body roles (paint, panel, trim, flat) RGB-only. The night
  env weight in vertex alpha is skipped for them, because it would move the alpha-test threshold. Their grime pass uses the Decal kind (masked by texture alpha), so cut-outs
  stay holes. Other roles with alpha test are rejected (`body_material_alpha_test_non_body_role`).
* Tests: cabin box (tail, floor and side triangles), cabin markers.
